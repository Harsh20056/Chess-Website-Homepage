THE

KING

The king in chess moves exactly one square in any direction: horizontally, vertically, or diagonally. It cannot move into check. The goal is to avoid capture; checkmate ends the game. Castling, a special move with a rook, enhances king safety early.

THE

QUEEN

The queen combines rook and bishop powers, moving any number of squares horizontally, vertically, or diagonally. The most powerful piece, it dominates open positions, controls the board, and often decides games through attacks and defense.

THE

BISHOP

The bishop moves any number of squares diagonally, staying on its starting color (light or dark). It controls long ranges, excels in open positions, and pairs effectively to dominate opposite-colored squares.

THE

KNIGHT

The knight leaps in an L-shape: two squares one way and one perpendicular, or one square and two. It jumps over pieces, changes color each move, and excels in closed positions and forks.

THE

ROOK

The rook moves any number of squares horizontally or vertically. It controls ranks and files, dominates open lines, and is crucial in endgames. Castling with the king enhances early safety.

THE

PAWN

The pawn advances one square forward, two from its starting rank. It captures diagonally. Promotes to queen, rook, bishop, or knight upon reaching the opponent’s back rank. En passant is a special capture.
